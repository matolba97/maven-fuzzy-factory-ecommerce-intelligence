## Conversion: investigate the largest observed losses

| Ordered stage | Distinct sessions | Conversion from prior | Sessions lost from prior |

The largest stage-to-stage proportional drop after product reach is product-to-cart: 63.65% of product-reaching sessions do not progress to cart. Investigate product-detail relevance, offer clarity, loading performance and acquisition intent. These are hypotheses for testing, not established causes. Listing and detail views are deliberately grouped into the observed Product stage; a future deeper funnel could separate them.

Desktop sessions convert at 8.50% versus 3.09% for mobile, a 5.41 percentage-point difference. Prioritize device-specific journey investigation, stratified by source/campaign and month. The aggregate difference is not a causal device effect and must not be presented as guaranteed recoverable revenue.

## Revenue: compare complete exposure periods

February 2015 recorded sales were $129,212.94, 95.74% above February 2014. Both are complete months. March 2015 ends on March 19 and should not be presented as a full-month collapse. The model blanks complete-month MoM/YoY comparisons at both extract edges.

## Product concentration and refunds

The Original Mr. Fuzzy contributes $1,211,057.74, 62.47% of recorded item sales. This creates observed revenue concentration; compare newer launches at comparable calendar ages before treating lower lifetime sales as weak product performance.

The Birthday Sugar Panda has the highest recorded sold-item refund incidence, 6.04% (301 of 4,985), compared with 5.11% for the Original Mr. Fuzzy. Investigate mature sale cohorts, campaign/device mix and refund reasons before attributing the difference to product quality. Refund reasons are absent. Recorded refunds occur 2–16 calendar days after sale in this extract; the latest sales have less follow-up, so these are observed rates rather than final defect rates.

## Customer behavior and cross-sell

591 of 31,696 observed buyer IDs made at least two recorded purchases (1.86%). This differs from repeat visitor sessions (78,553). Evaluate post-purchase retention with mature first-purchase cohorts; do not infer lifetime loyalty, merged people or churn from a bounded user-ID extract.

7,712 orders contain more than one item (23.87% of orders). Use primary vs additional-item economics and observed basket combinations to propose merchandising experiments. No causal uplift is established by co-purchase frequency.

## Acquisition decisions and evidence limits

Tagged search supplies 378,858 of 472,871 sessions (80.12%) and $1,544,817.39 in acquired-session sales. This indicates dependence on this observed source group, not verified paid-media ROI. Campaign labels and referrers support traffic segmentation but no advertising spend, ROAS, CAC or budget allocation can be calculated. Require actual cost and attribution data before recommending spend changes.

## Recommended next work

1. Inspect mobile and product-to-cart paths within matched month/channel cohorts, then design randomized tests where feasible.
2. Review Panda refund incidence on equally mature sale cohorts; obtain refund reasons and operational context.
3. Compare launch-age performance and additional-item margins before proposing basket placement experiments.
4. Connect actual marketing costs before moving from session efficiency to investment decisions.
5. Expand customer observation and identity documentation before a retention program business case.
